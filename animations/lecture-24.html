Generation and retrieval-augmented systems

  • 1 · Retrieval-augmented generation, and its join

1Retrieval-augmented generation, and its join

A twelve-entry catalogue and a question. Step 1 embeds the question and scores every entry by cosine; step 2 keeps the top $k$; step 3 puts them into the prompt, in rank order, until the context budget runs out; step 4 generates an answer that cites stock numbers; step 5 checks it. The generator is a stand-in that reads perfectly — it recommends exactly the entries a person judged relevant — so every failure you see belongs to the join. Changing a control re-runs the whole pipeline.

The mathematics

The toy encoder gives each word a fixed vector and a text the normalised mean of its words' vectors (mean pooling; words like to and the are dropped). The score is the cosine $s_j = \mathbf{q}^{\top}\mathbf{e}_j$ of two unit vectors.

The retriever's recall at $k$ is a ceiling for the system: if no relevant entry is in the top $k$, none reaches the generator, so for a question that has an answer

$$\begin{aligned}\Pr(\text{correct}) &\le \Pr(\text{a relevant entry}\\ &\qquad\text{reaches the generator})\\ &\le \text{recall@}k .\end{aligned}$$

Grounding is the fraction of cited stock numbers that exist. It measures whether an answer was built from the catalogue, not whether it is right.

Watch for
  • A distractor that shares words with the question outranks the right entry: Rain gauge, logs every shower of rain is first for something to keep the rain off.
  • With a small budget, a long entry near the top fills the context and the right entries are cut, although the retriever found them: recall@$k$ looks fine and the answer is wrong.
  • An answer citing a real but wrong entry scores 100% on grounding. Grounding is not correctness.
