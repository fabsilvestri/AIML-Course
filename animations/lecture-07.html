Ensembles and random forests

1The variance of an average

$n$ predictors of the same quantity, each with variance $\sigma^2 = 1$ and pairwise correlation $\rho$. Each step is one draw: all $n$ predictions at once, and their average $\bar f$. On the left, the latest draws, one per row; on the right, the spread of a single predictor against the spread of the average over every draw so far. Below, the law itself: the variance of the average against $n$.

The mathematics

The variance of a sum is the sum of every covariance, $\operatorname{Var}(\sum_t f_t) = \sum_s\sum_t \operatorname{Cov}(f_s, f_t)$: $n$ diagonal terms equal to $\sigma^2$ and $n(n-1)$ off-diagonal ones equal to $\rho\sigma^2$. Dividing by $n^2$,

$$\operatorname{Var}(\bar f) = \rho\sigma^2 + \frac{(1-\rho)\,\sigma^2}{n}.$$

The draws are built to have exactly that structure: with $Z$ and $\varepsilon_1, \dots, \varepsilon_n$ independent standard normals, $f_t = \sigma\bigl(\sqrt\rho\,Z + \sqrt{1-\rho}\,\varepsilon_t\bigr)$ has variance $\sigma^2(\rho + 1 - \rho) = \sigma^2$, and two of them share only $Z$, so $\operatorname{Cov}(f_s, f_t) = \rho\sigma^2$.

A variance estimated from $N$ draws of a normal quantity has standard error about $V\sqrt{2/N}$; the side panel prints it.

Watch for
  • In each row the $n$ dots cluster around a common centre, the shared part $\sqrt\rho\,Z$, and that centre moves from row to row. Averaging pulls $\bar f$ to the centre; it cannot hold the centre still.
  • The histogram of $\bar f$ is narrower than that of one predictor, but not as narrow as $\sigma^2/n$ would make it.
  • The law falls steeply for the first few members and then flattens out just above the floor $\rho\sigma^2$, which it never crosses.
Try this
  1. Set $\rho = 0$ and $n = 50$: the variance of the average drops to $1/50 = 0.02$, the classical result.
  2. Set $\rho = 1$: every row collapses to a single point and the average is exactly as variable as one predictor.
  3. With $\rho = 0.3$, compare $n = 10$ and $n = 50$. Five times the members buys a variance of $0.37$ instead of $0.314$.

2Bagging and random forests

Two classes with a curved boundary and 10% of the labels flipped. Each step draws a bootstrap sample — 300 rows drawn with replacement from the 300 training rows — grows an unconstrained tree on it, and adds it to the vote. The shading is the ensemble's vote share; the dashed line is the boundary of the tree just added, and the hollow points are its out-of-bag rows. On the right, test error and out-of-bag error as the ensemble grows.

The mathematics

A given row is missed by one draw with probability $1 - 1/m$, so by all $m$ draws of a bootstrap sample with probability

$$\Bigl(1 - \frac1m\Bigr)^{m} \;\longrightarrow\; e^{-1} \approx 0.368,$$

using $\lim_{m\to\infty}(1 + x/m)^m = e^{x}$ with $x = -1$. For $m = 300$ the exact value is $0.367$. Each row's out-of-bag prediction is the majority vote of the trees that never saw it, and the out-of-bag error is the error of those predictions.

A random forest also draws, at every node, which features may be split on. With two features and one allowed per node, each node splits on a feature chosen at random — a worse tree, and a less correlated one: $\rho$ falls in $\rho\sigma^2 + (1-\rho)\sigma^2/n$.

Watch for
  • Each new tree's dashed boundary is jagged and chases flipped labels. The ensemble's boundary smooths as trees are added.
  • The ensemble's test error falls about five points below a single tree's within the first twenty or so trees, and then barely moves.
  • The out-of-bag error follows the test error's shape without a single held-out row, ending about two points below it — an estimate from 300 rows, whose standard error is itself about two points.
Try this
  1. Play to 100 trees with bagging, then switch to the random forest and play again. The forest's trees are a little worse on their own (mean error 23.1% against 22.7%), and the forest is better (16.0% against 17.8%): a lower $\rho$ bought with a slightly larger $\sigma^2$. On CoverType the same trade went the other way.
  2. Read the out-of-bag share in the side panel as trees are added: it wanders between about 0.34 and 0.40, around $0.367$.

3AdaBoost with stumps

A decision stump — a tree of depth 1 — can draw one straight, axis-aligned line, and these two classes need a curved boundary. Each step fits a stump to the weighted data, gives it a vote $\alpha_j$ by its weighted error, and multiplies the weight of every point it got wrong by $e^{\alpha_j}$. Point size is weight. The shading is the weighted vote of every stump so far.

The mathematics

The book's AdaBoost, with weights $w^{(i)}$ starting at $1/m$. The $j$-th stump's weighted error rate and vote are

$$r_j = \frac{\sum_{\hat y_j^{(i)} \ne y^{(i)}} w^{(i)}}{\sum_i w^{(i)}}, \qquad \alpha_j = \eta\,\log\frac{1 - r_j}{r_j},$$

with $\eta$ the learning rate. Every misclassified point's weight is multiplied by $e^{\alpha_j}$, then all weights are divided by their sum. A prediction is the class with the largest total vote, $\arg\max_k \sum_{j:\,\hat y_j(\mathbf x) = k} \alpha_j$.

$\alpha_j > 0$ exactly when $r_j < \tfrac12$, and it grows without limit as $r_j \to 0$. Each stump is the axis-aligned threshold with the smallest weighted error $r_j$, each side predicting the class that carries more of its weight. (Scikit-learn's stumps minimise the weighted Gini impurity instead; the idea is the same.)

Watch for
  • The points a stump gets wrong swell, so the next stump is drawn towards them. The same few thresholds come back again and again, each time with a different weight on the points around them.
  • Each new stump's weighted error $r_j$ is measured on the reweighted data, so later stumps have errors nearer $\tfrac12$ and smaller votes.
  • The ensemble's boundary, the thin line, is built only from axis-aligned pieces: a staircase that climbs with the parabola's arms wherever training points pin it down.
Try this
  1. Step five times and read $r_j$ and $\alpha_j$ in the narration each time.
  2. Lower $\eta$ to 0.2 and replay: the weights change more slowly, and the training error takes more rounds to fall.

4Gradient boosting

Sixty noisy points from a smooth curve. The ensemble starts as the mean of the targets. Each stage fits a small regression tree to the current residuals — what the ensemble still gets wrong — and adds it, scaled by the learning rate $\eta$. On the left, the data and the ensemble; on the right, the residuals and the tree fitted to them. Below, training and test error stage by stage.

The mathematics

With $F_0 = \bar y$, stage $j$ computes the residuals $r^{(i)} = y^{(i)} - F_{j-1}(\mathbf x^{(i)})$, fits a regression tree $h_j$ to them, and sets

$$F_j = F_{j-1} + \eta\,h_j .$$

For the squared error $\tfrac12(y - F)^2$ the residual is exactly the negative gradient with respect to the prediction, $-\partial/\partial F\;\tfrac12(y - F)^2 = y - F$: each stage is one step of gradient descent, taken in the space of functions, with the tree as the step direction. The learning rate is the shrinkage: each tree corrects only a fraction $\eta$ of what it found.

Training error cannot rise. A least-squares tree's leaf values are means of the residuals in each leaf, so $\langle r, h\rangle = \lVert h\rVert^2$ over the training points, and

$$\lVert r - \eta h\rVert^2 = \lVert r\rVert^2 - (2\eta - \eta^2)\lVert h\rVert^2 \le \lVert r\rVert^2$$

for every $0 < \eta \le 2$.

Watch for
  • The residuals on the right lose their shape. At first they trace the part of the curve the ensemble has missed; within a few dozen stages they are a structureless band about zero, about as wide as the noise, and each new tree is fitting that noise.
  • Training error falls at every stage. Test error falls, bottoms out at stage 13, and then creeps back up — from 0.127 to 0.168 by stage 300. Unlike bagging, more members can hurt.
Try this
  1. Set $\eta = 1$, no shrinkage, and play to 300. The lowest test error is 0.140, at stage 7, and by stage 300 it has climbed to 0.195.
  2. Set $\eta = 0.1$ and replay. The minimum comes much later, at stage 82, and is lower than with $\eta = 1$: 0.127; at stage 300 the test error is still only 0.142. Shrinkage needs more members and overfits more slowly.
  3. Switch to depth 2 at $\eta = 0.5$: each tree is more flexible, the training error reaches almost zero, and the test error ends at 0.230.
